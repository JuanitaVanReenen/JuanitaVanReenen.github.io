Small Business Record-Keeping Basics: Orders, Stock, Expenses and Pricing

A straightforward record system for independent sellers, makers and small online shops.

When a small business is busy, important details can end up in messages, notebooks and memory. A simple record-keeping routine helps bring the essentials together. You do not need a complicated system to begin; you need a few consistent records and a regular time to review them.

1. Keep an order record

For each order, record the date, order reference, product, quantity, amount charged, payment status and fulfilment status. Keep customer information only where you genuinely need it and protect it appropriately. A clear order list helps you see what is waiting to be packed, delivered or followed up.

2. Track stock and reorder points

Record each product or material, opening quantity, stock received, quantity sold or used, current quantity and a reorder level. Review fast-moving items more often. A reorder point is a reminder to check supplier lead times before stock runs out; it is not a guarantee that a supplier will have stock available.

3. Record business expenses

Keep the date, supplier, description, category, amount and receipt reference for each business expense. Separate business and personal spending where possible, and store supporting documents in an organised place. Check local rules about what records you must keep and for how long.

4. Understand the cost behind each product

Before setting a selling price, list the direct materials, packaging, labour, overhead and selling fees that apply. A price that covers materials alone may not cover the time and other costs involved. Review the assumptions whenever supplier prices, platform fees or production methods change.

5. Review the numbers every week

Set aside a short weekly review. Check open orders, overdue payments, low stock, recent expenses and products whose costs have changed. At month-end, compare income and spending records and file receipts. A consistent routine is more useful than a large spreadsheet that is rarely updated.

6. Make your spreadsheet easy to maintain

Use clear column headings, one row per transaction and consistent date and currency formats. Avoid merging cells in data tables. Keep a backup copy and test formulas with sample entries before relying on a calculator or dashboard. If you use a CSV file, open it in compatible spreadsheet software and check that columns display correctly.

Common questions

What records should a small online shop keep?
At a minimum, keep useful records of sales and orders, inventory, expenses, supplier purchases and product costs. Legal and tax record requirements vary by country, so check local guidance.

Can I use CSV templates in Excel or Google Sheets?
Yes. CSV files can be opened or imported into compatible spreadsheet applications. Check the column layout and save your working copy in the format recommended by your spreadsheet app.

Does a tracker replace bookkeeping or tax advice?
No. A tracker helps organise information. It is not accounting, tax or legal advice and does not determine which records your local rules require.
